You Will Not Die Again

The third time he died was the last. His family made sure of it.

His first death came as a child. On his first day of school, his teacher found him in the playground, purple and rigid, his face covered in saliva from his mouth and nose. The doctor pronounced him dead, and that afternoon he was buried. A couple of hours later, with his relatives still near the tomb, he awoke from a cataleptic seizure. His cries could be heard blocks away. They dug him out with their hands. From then on, everyone called him Lazarus.

His second death came years later. Lazarus had been missing for almost a week when a body was found decomposing in a sugarcane field outside the village. There was little doubt it was him: the body wore his clothes, his cap, his prescription glasses.

A week after the burial, Lazarus reappeared in rags, staring blankly, speaking incoherently. The same police who had declared him dead determined he had been drugged with scopolamine and robbed of everything, even the clothes on his back. The dead man had been the thief.

The third death left no room for doubt. It happened at home, the body was watched the entire time, an autopsy and a DNA test were performed, and the funeral lasted a whole week. Despite the immense love his family and friends had for him, they all felt relieved knowing that this time they would not have to bury him again. They said goodbye without a single tear. They had already shed every one they had for him.

To make sure there would not be another burial, they cremated him and divided his ashes among themselves in small bags, as if afraid the ashes might come together and bring him back.

Even so, whenever someone knocks at the door, they all have the feeling that it is Lazarus, returned from the beyond to die once more.
