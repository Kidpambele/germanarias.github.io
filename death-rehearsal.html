Death Rehearsal

We all knew he wasn’t really dead. He had arranged his own funeral, convinced that only after confronting the end of his life could he begin again.

We found him lying in the coffin, with tufts of cotton in his nostrils and surrounded by the flowers we had sent. As he lay motionless, some of us offered words of farewell while others recalled moments we had shared. Although we understood the purpose of the ceremony, it soon stopped feeling as though we were pretending.

The sobbing began during the procession to the cemetery. When the empty coffin disappeared behind the crematorium doors, many of us wept as if we had truly lost him.

Two hours later, he emerged carrying a small urn containing what were meant to be his own ashes. We accompanied him to the river where we had swum as children, and there he scattered them in silence.

We never spoke of that funeral again. There was no need. From that day forward, he let go of old grudges, rekindled neglected relationships, and made decisions he had postponed for years.
