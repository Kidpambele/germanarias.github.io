A Pessimistic Diagnosis

Every doctor he consulted, each more distinguished than the last, confirmed his family physician’s diagnosis: he had barely two months to live. More out of stubbornness than optimism, he refused to accept his fate and devoted his time and fortune to proving them wrong.

After exhausting the opinions of specialists and every unconventional treatment, he bought every book he could find about his illness, convinced that he would discover something everyone else had overlooked. When he found nothing, he turned to a clairvoyant renowned for predicting—and altering—the future. Although he had always placed his faith solely in science, he heard so many stories of miraculous recoveries that he became convinced he had finally found the answer.

One glance into his eyes was enough for the clairvoyant to declare the diagnosis incorrect. She gave him two potions and promised they would cure him. After drinking them, he felt better than ever. He showered her with expensive gifts, resumed his projects, and died happy that very night.

The autopsy confirmed that he had an incurable disease. It also revealed that he had not died from it, but from the clairvoyant’s potions.
